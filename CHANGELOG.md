# Changelog

All notable changes to Schematic Amp Sim. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the
version numbers follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (see [docs/RELEASING.md](docs/RELEASING.md)).

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- **The RAT overdrive model runs at four times the host rate** (it was twice): its hard clipping folded harmonics back as an inharmonic fizz about 45 dB under the note (about 43 dB with TONE open); it is now about 59 dB under (54 with TONE open). Costs about 8 % of a core instead of 4.5 %.

### Added
- **British 45** (amp `jtm45`): the Marshall JTM45 (1962-1966), from the Marshall "basic schematic for trem amps" drawing (types 1961, 1962 and 1987/T). It is the Fender Bassman 5F6-A circuit with a British valve set: ECC83 in the preamp (more gain than the Bassman's 12AY7), two KT66 (run as the engine's 6L6GC; the EL34 and the 6L6GC are the options), GZ34 rectifier, 470 ohm screen resistors and 22k grid stoppers. Bright and normal channels with the Bassman's jacks (bright 1 and 2, normal 1 and 2, jumpered), bass, middle, treble and presence. Assumptions in the netlist: 100k plate loads on V1 (the drawing prints 180k and 100k), a 6k6 output transformer (no value in the drawing), the tremolo of the trem chassis left out.
- **Distortion module, second model: Boss MT-2 style** (approximate). The gain stages come from the Boss drawing (an op-amp stage with a bass-heavy gain, the DIST stage up to 48 dB, 1SS133 diode clipping and a last stage with a treble lift; engine/data/mt2.net), run at four times the host rate. The active EQ is done with filters after the circuit, in the ranges of the pedal: LOW and HIGH (+-15 dB at 100 Hz and 10 kHz), MIDDLE (+-15 dB) with MID FREQ sweeping it from 200 Hz to 5 kHz; LEVEL is applied there too. The three transistors of the drawing (Q007, Q008, Q010) are emitter followers that bootstrap the RC networks at the inputs of the gain stages, and are in the circuit as such; the EQ and the level are not circuits but filters, which is why the model is called approximate. 4 presets.
- **Distortion module** (rack module `Distortion`, Effects category), the pedals that are neither an overdrive nor a fuzz, solved as circuits like the overdrive models. The first model is a **Boss DS-1** (engine/data/ds1.net, transcribed from the Boss service drawing of the Japanese board): a 2SC2240 buffer and booster, the TA7136 gain stage with the DIST pot (gain from 1 to 22), hard diode clipping, the passive tone (a low-pass and a high-pass mixed: a scooped middle) and the level. Controls MODEL, DIST, TONE, LEVEL; 5 presets. It runs at four times the host rate, like the RAT.
- **Sustain layers** (rack module `Layers`, Space category), in the manner of the MXR Layers: it takes a snapshot of what you play and holds it as a layer, a soft pad that sustains under what comes next; three layers at once. A layer starts on a pick attack over the TRIG threshold (TRIG at 0: never) or by hand with the HOLD LAYER switch (held while it is on, faded out when it is released). ATTACK (0.02 to 3 s) and DECAY (0.3 to 14 s) set the fade in and out, and the highs fade first, as on a string. SINGLE keeps one layer; SUB OCTAVE makes the new layers an octave lower; DIFFUSION smears them with all-pass filters; CHANNELS spreads the three layers left, centre and right. Each layer is the snapshot played as overlapping grains that follow one another by a least-squares match, so a held note stays steady. 7 presets. (The Layers' modulation and delay settings are not in this version.)
- **The Compressor module has a MODEL choice**: the Keeley Compressor Plus circuit as before, plus three more voicings that use the same panel (Sustain, Blend, Tone, Level, Fast): **Dyna Comp** (a fast peak detector and a high ratio: the squeeze and the "quack" of funk and country), **FET** (a very fast attack, a ratio that rises with Sustain, a touch of saturation: punchy, in the manner of the 1176) and **Opto** (slow, a soft knee, a release that depends on how long it has been compressing: smooth, in the manner of the LA-2A). Only the Keeley model is run inside the amp when the compressor sits next to it; the others are modules of their own. 7 new presets; the old ones keep the Keeley model.
- **Bitcrusher / lo-fi** (rack module `Bitcrusher`, Lo-fi category): it throws away resolution and sampling rate, as the first samplers, consoles and telephone codecs did. BITS (1 to 16, fractions work), RATE (a sample-and-hold from 500 Hz up; what is above half of it folds back as aliasing), ANTI-ALIAS (a low-pass in front of the hold: duller and cleaner), TONE (a low-pass after it), DITHER (a little noise before the rounding, which turns the quantising distortion into a steady hiss), JITTER (the hold's clock wobbles, a rougher grain) and MIX. 7 presets.
- **BBD analog delay** (rack module `BbdDelay`, Space category), in the manner of the Boss DM-2, the Memory Man and the Carbon Copy: a bucket-brigade chip whose bandwidth follows its clock, so the longer the delay the duller the repeats, and every repeat is a little darker than the one before. Controls TIME (40 to 600 ms), FEEDBACK (past 100 % the loop builds up and saturates, still bounded), MIX, TONE, MOD RATE and MOD DEPTH (the clock modulation: a chorus inside the delay; turning the time glides the pitch), GRIT (soft saturation and a hiss that breathes with the signal) and CHANNELS (in stereo the right channel's modulation is a quarter cycle after the left's). 7 presets.
- **Harmonizer** (rack module `Harmonist`, Pitch category, in the manner of the Boss PS-6 Harmonist): two voices made from the note you play and mixed with the guitar. Modes **Harmony** (each voice is an interval counted in steps of a scale in the chosen key: +2 is a third above, +4 a fifth above, -2 a third below; the interval is major or minor as the scale asks; scales Major, Minor, Harmonic minor, Dorian and Mixolydian; a pitch detector finds the note, so this mode is monophonic), **Pitch** (each voice a fixed number of semitones, -12 to +12; chords work) and **Detune** (one voice a few cents sharp and the other as many flat). Controls MODE, KEY, SCALE, INTERVAL 1 and 2, DETUNE, DIRECT, VOICE 1 and 2; 8 presets. The voices come from the rack's pitch shifter with a longer grain (40 ms, so the low strings stay whole); the guitar is delayed by the same time to line up with them, so it adds about 24 ms of latency while it is on.
- **The wah has a FILTER choice** (band-pass, low-pass, high-pass): with the envelope or the LFO it works as a dynamic filter and auto-wah, a low-pass that every note throws open (the funk "quack") or a high-pass that thins the sound as it opens. 4 new presets (Envelope low-pass, Low-pass duck, Envelope high-pass, Low-pass sweep).
- **Analog octaver** (rack module `Octaver`, Pitch category, in the manner of the Boss OC-2): the guitar goes through a tracking low-pass filter, a Schmitt comparator squares it, two flip-flops divide the square wave by 2 and 4, and each octave is given the loudness of the playing and rounded by a filter that follows the note; mixed with the direct signal. Controls DIRECT, OCTAVE 1 (one octave down), OCTAVE 2 (two octaves down) and TONE (how much of the square wave's buzz the octaves keep); 5 presets. Monophonic like the pedal: it tracks one note at a time (the highest notes above about 1 kHz and chords make it warble). Costs almost nothing in CPU.
- **Ten-band graphic EQ pedal** (rack module `TenBandEq`, EQ category, in the manner of the MXR ten-band EQ): ten sliders an octave apart (31.25 Hz to 16 kHz), +-12 dB each (octave-wide peaking filters, Q 1.41, so neighbours overlap), a GAIN in front of the filters and a VOLUME at the output (+-12 dB each), 9 presets (Flat, Smile, Lead mids, Tight metal, Warm jazz, Glassy clean, Solo boost, Bass punch and Off). It works on a mono or stereo signal, fades over 20 ms when switched and costs almost nothing (ten biquads; nothing when every slider is flat). Placed in front of the amp by default like the other pedals; it can be moved anywhere in the rack. It is a behavioural model (the pedal's own bandpass sections and their interaction are not modelled) and was not compared with a real pedal. The editor has ten vertical sliders (double click for 0 dB). Parameters `ten_on`, `ten_b1` .. `ten_b10`, `ten_gain`, `ten_volume`; 14 values per preset (`PresetValue v[14]`).
- **The Overdrive module has four models** (a MODEL selector, parameter `ts_model`; projects saved before it open with the Tube Screamer): the **Tube Screamer** (the analytic model, as before; it is the one the amp runs when it sits right before it), an **SD-1 style** pedal, a **BD-2 style** pedal and a **RAT style** pedal, all three solved as circuits at twice the host rate (`engine/data/sd1.net`, `engine/data/bd2.net`, `engine/data/rat.net`, `sas/drive_circuit.h`) and both with DRIVE, TONE and LEVEL like the Tube Screamer, nine new presets (3 per new model). The SD-1 comes from a service-manual style drawing with all values printed: an input buffer, a 4558 gain stage with DRIVE (1M) and R5 (33k) in the feedback and asymmetrical clipping (one diode against two), the R7 / C4 low pass, the active tone stage (20k) and LEVEL (10k); the diode type is not printed (1N4148-like assumed). The BD-2 comes from a hobbyist redraw of the 2001 schematic: a JFET input buffer, two discrete gain stages (a JFET pair into a PNP, modelled as an op-amp with an open-loop gain of about 700 and a 2.2k output: assumed) with the dual-gang GAIN (2 x 250k) in their feedback, a four-diode clipper between them, the passive TONE (10k), LEVEL (100k log), an op-amp stage with a gyrator and the output buffer; the on / off switching is left out and the bottom end of the GAIN pots is read as open. The RAT comes from the General Guitar Gadgets "Rodent" drawing (all values printed): an LM308 gain stage (non-inverting, DISTORTION 100k log in its feedback with 100p, 560 + 4.7u and 47 + 2.2u in the inverting leg: gain 1 to about 2300), 1N914 diodes to ground after the output coupling, the FILTER (100k log with 1.5k and 3.3n) and a JFET follower with VOLUME; TONE is the reverse of the pedal's FILTER (clockwise brighter). The LM308 is the gain-bandwidth limited element (about 1 MHz); its slew limit is not modelled and the JFET is an ideal follower. Not compared with the real pedals or by ear. Cost at 2x: about 6 % of a core (SD-1), 8 % (RAT) and 15 % (BD-2) in the benchmark of this machine (noisy), against 1 % for the Tube Screamer. The Klon-style drive is still a module of its own.
- **"German Ultra"** (a 120 W two-channel EL34 head, from a redrawn schematic of the preamp and of the power amp with the DC voltages of the real amp written on it): a shared first stage (V1a) and two channels picked by the optocouplers and the relay (selector CHANNEL): **Overdrive** (V1b, V2b, V3a and the V3b cathode follower, tone stack with GAIN, TREBLE, MIDDLE, BASS and VOLUME) and **Clean** (V4b, its own tone stack with GAIN, TREBLE, MIDDLE, BASS, PRESENCE and VOLUME), then V5a and V5b with the effects loop in bypass and the MASTER; a long-tailed ECC83 phase inverter with the negative feedback and PRESENCE into its feedback node (with the .422 H resonance network), four EL34 on fixed bias (-47 V). The clean channel's controls are six new parameters (`clean_gain`, `clean_treble`, `clean_middle`, `clean_bass`, `clean_presence`, `clean_volume`). The clean channel runs with the power amp's negative feedback open, as the real amp does: a selector position can now set the power amp's feedback resistor (`nfb_ohms` in amp.json; the Lite power amp has no feedback, so it ignores it). The DC points of the model match the voltages written on the schematic within a few volts (V1b plate 230 V, V3a plate 183 V, V3b cathode 184 V, V5b plate 267 V against 261 V). Assumed or not modelled: the effects loop and the line output; R30 (labelled 180k) is 100k because the voltages on the drawing need it; the transformer tap of the feedback is the 4 ohm one; the clean channel is much quieter than the Overdrive channel (as the drawing's levels say), so its presets have the volume and master at the top. The preamp is solved in five stages (the guitar and V1a with the input networks, the Overdrive channel's tubes, its tone stack, the clean channel, and the output stages with the master), cut at coupling points so the sound is the same within 0.3 dB: it costs about 40 % of a core in Normal at 2x instead of about 80 % for a single netlist. Levelled by its peak with hot guitar signals (the Overdrive channel at -4 dBFS); not listened to.
- **"British Dual 100"** (a 100 W two-channel EL34 head of the 2000s, from the factory drawings of the valve board JCM2-60-02 and the controls board JCM2-61-00, replacing the "British 900 SL"): V1b input stage, a relay that picks the **Classic** channel (its network with GAIN, then V2b directly) or the **Ultra** channel (its network with GAIN into V1a, then V2b), V2a, V3b and a V3a cathode follower into the Marshall-type tone stack (BASS, MIDDLE, TREBLE), a volume for each channel (CLASSIC VOLUME, ULTRA VOLUME), the mute relay and the buffer; a long-tailed ECC83 phase inverter, four EL34 on fixed bias and PRESENCE. Selectors CHANNEL (Classic / Ultra), CLASSIC MODE (A / B: the SW1 switches, B has more gain) and ULTRA MODE (A / B: the SW3 switches, B has more gain); 4 presets. Parameters: `volume2` is CLASSIC GAIN, `volume` CLASSIC VOLUME, `clean_gain` ULTRA GAIN and `clean_volume` ULTRA VOLUME. The preamp is solved in three stages (V1 with both networks, V2a and V3, the tone stack and volumes), cut at the V2b plate and at the cathode follower; the sound matches the single netlist and the cost in Normal at 2x is about 24 to 30 % of a core (Lite about 16 to 21 %, Eco about 12 to 15 %). Assumed or unreadable on the scans: C27 (22n), the exact wiring of the supply rails (R61, R19, R24, R28 as a tree), the mode names (A / B are the switch positions, not the labels of the front panel), the output-transformer constants (the C3070 is modelled like the other 100 W amps), the negative feedback (a single 47k from the 4 ohm tap into the phase inverter tail; the op-amp stage between them is not modelled) and the bias supply (-48 V). Not modelled: the effects loop, the reverb, the footswitch and relay drivers, the tone-shift switch SW4 (left as drawn), the JFET mutes and the LDR gain option. The Lite power amp is fitted to the circuit within about 6 dB at the extremes of the presence knob (3 dB or less elsewhere). Levelled by its peak with hot guitar signals (the loudest presets at -4 dBFS); not listened to.
- **"British 900"** (a 100 W single-channel EL34 head, the Marshall JCM900 Master Volume 2100 / 2500 of 1990, from the factory drawings CD0109 issue 9 (preamp) and CD0192 issue 7 (power amp)): V1a, V1b with the GAIN 1 pot (`volume`) at its grid, V2a behind GAIN 2 (`volume2`) and the three-diode clipping network (BR1 + D2), the V2b cathode follower, the Marshall-type tone stack, the master volume (the op-amp section modelled as an ideal inverting stage), a long-tailed ECC83 phase inverter with the 27k feedback and PRESENCE, four EL34 on fixed bias; 4 presets. Assumed: how VR1 and VR2 are wired (read from the drawing), the output transformer, the HT and the bias figures (copied from the JCM800, not on the drawings). Not modelled: the effects loop, the input zeners and the bias supply (a fixed bias). Levelled by the loudness match; not listened to.
- **Linux build** (x86_64): VST3 and standalone application, built and tested by GitHub Actions on Ubuntu (a `linux` job in `ci.yml` and `release.yml`, `package-linux.sh`, the `release-linux` CMake preset), packaged as a `.tar.gz`. It has not been tried by ear in a Linux DAW.

### Changed
- **The Leslie has a WIDTH knob** (parameter `les_width`, 7 by default, in the presets): it opens the two microphones apart (15 to 90 degrees each side of the front, 30 to 180 between them; it was fixed at 120) and narrows the horn's beam, so at full width the left and right channels swell against each other (the level correlation between the channels goes from +0.8 to -0.5 or lower, `tests/test_leslie_width.cpp`) and at 0 the output is nearly mono. The level stays within a few dB. The six presets now set it (Chorale 7, Tremolo 8, Gentle 5, Deep and bright 9, Warm rotor 7, Brake 7).
- **The Acoustic Sim shapes the sound more**: the body resonances reach 2.6 times the table at full BODY (1.6 before) and are a quarter wider, so a dreadnought now spans about 11 dB between its low-mid scoop (-6.5 dB at 500 Hz) and its upper-mid presence (+4.8 dB at 3.5 kHz) instead of 7 dB, and the piezo type lifts 3.5 kHz by 11.5 dB (6.7 before); the level is still kept (`sas_acoustic_check`). The knobs and their ranges are the same.
- **The American Clean 120 is 10 dB louder** (`output_db` 12 -> 22): it was levelled by its peak with hot guitar signals (0.55 V), so at a normal guitar level and the default knobs it played 17 dB below the other amps at the reference input. It now sits about 7 dB below the reference at the defaults (it has more headroom than the others, so it stays quieter at the same knob position); a very hot input can now go past full scale at the output (the chain has no limiter after the amp). The `sas_amp_response` tool prints an amp's frequency response for the given knob settings.
- **Lite uses the triode tables and the factorisation reuse of Normal** (it solved the preamp with the device formulas, so for preamp-heavy amps it cost as much as Normal or more: the American 5153 was 108 % against 99 % in Normal). Same circuit and the same tables as Normal; Lite now costs about 74 % (5153), 42 % (German Ultra), 32 % (Hiwatt) and 35 % (Lead C+) of a core in the benchmark (noisy), and Eco is no longer cheaper than Lite except where an amp has its own finer Eco split. Not compared by ear with the old Lite.
- **The American Lead C+ preamp is solved in three stages** (the guitar, V1A, the tone stack and V1B; the rhythm and lead paths with V3B, V4A, V2B, the lead master and the effects loop divider; V2A with the master), cut at V1B's coupling capacitor and at V2A's grid: the level matches the single netlist within 0.05 dB and the worst octave band within 0.4 dB (`sas_split_check lead_c_plus`, which now has Lead C+ cases). Normal costs about 70 to 80 % instead of 85 % and Lite about 34 % instead of 50 %.
- **Lighter high-gain amps.** The preamp of the American 5153 is solved in seven stages instead of three (cut at the coupling capacitors and the cathode followers, each stage with the supply decoupling of the whole preamp and dummy loads for the currents of the others), and the German Ultra's in five: the sound is the same within about 0.3 dB (0.7 dB at the hottest settings) and the cost in Normal at 2x goes from about 77 % to about 50 % of a core for the 5153 and from 78 % to about 30 % for the German Ultra (the benchmark is noisy while other programs compile).

## [0.4.0-beta] - 2026-11-08

### Added
- **The amp list is grouped by character**: clean amps, crunch amps, high-gain amps, bass amps and other (the NAM loader), each group in alphabetical order, with a header for each group (amp.json `category`; the search still works and hides the empty groups). The `order` of each amp, which saved projects store, did not change.
- **Leslie** (rotary speaker, Modulation) and **Spring reverb** (Space), two new modules of the rack. The Leslie splits the sound at 800 Hz into a horn and a rotor that turn at their own rates (Slow 0.8 / 0.67 Hz, Fast 6.7 / 5.8 Hz, with the horn and the heavy drum gliding to the new speed in about 1 and 3 seconds; Stop is the brake), two microphones 120 degrees apart hear the Doppler and the tremolo, and the output is stereo; knobs SPEED, DEPTH, HORN / ROTOR and MIX, 7 presets (`engine/include/sas/leslie.h`). The spring reverb is the behavioural tank of the amps (`sas/spring.h`) with a left and a right tank of slightly different lengths, three tanks (Short, Medium, Long), TONE and MIX, 7 presets (`engine/include/sas/spring_reverb.h`). Both fade over 20 ms when switched; the spring's tail does not ring out. Tools: `sas_klon_check`, `sas_leslie_spring_check`.
- **Klon-style overdrive**, a new pedal of the rack (Effects, **KLON-STYLE DRIVE**), solved as a circuit from the ElectroSmash drawing of the Klon Centaur: the input buffer, the gain stage (non-inverting, gain 4.6 to 26 with a mid hump) into two germanium diodes, the clean path that stays in the mix through the second half of the dual GAIN pot, the summing amplifier, the tone stage and the level. Knobs GAIN, TREBLE and LEVEL, 6 presets; it runs at twice the host rate and loads the guitar like the other pedals. The charge pump is not modelled (the rails are fixed) and the diodes are an assumed 1N34A-like model; not compared with a real pedal.
- **Graphic equaliser inside an amp** (`graphic_eq` in amp.json, `sas/graphic_eq.h`): fixed bands, each a peaking filter with a slider (plugin parameters `aeq1..5`, 5 is flat, up to the band's `range_db`), applied at the output of the amp before the speaker, so it works in every quality. The American Lead C+ has the Mark's five bands (60 Hz, 240 Hz, 750 Hz, 2.2 kHz and 6.6 kHz, +-12 dB, one octave wide; the drawn values are not read from the original).
- **"American 5153" (a three-channel 100 W high-gain head)**, from the user's service drawing (a scan): the Green, Blue and Red channels as the relay states K1 / K2 / K3 (the amp's CHANNEL selector), the shared V1B / V2A / V2B / V3A / V3B chain with the two gain networks and the two tone stacks, channel Three's own V1A / V4 / V5 / V6 chain and stack, the effects loop's buffers V7A / V7B (closed), the long-tailed V8 inverter and four 6L6GC (80 mV across a 1 ohm cathode resistor sets the bias: about 24 mA a tube here), the 16 / 8 / 4 ohm transformer. One set of knobs (GAIN, LOW, MID, HIGH, VOLUME, PRESENCE) drives the pots of the channel in use together (the three channels' pots are not the same size, so LOW and MID are only close). The presence / resonance board is on another sheet: the feedback network is assumed and the resonance control is not modelled; the muting JFETs and zeners are left out. The Lite mode carries the JCM800's block.
- **"American Clean 120" (a 1980s two-channel solid-state combo, the Jazz Chorus kind)**, from the user's drawing: the High / Low jacks (12 dB apart), the IC2b and IC2a stages with the DISTORTION pot and its switch, two diode clippers, the passive tone stack (TREBLE, BASS, MIDDLE, with the HI-TREBLE network across the VOLUME), IC3a and IC3b, solved as a circuit with 4558-type op-amps on +-15 V; the 60 W direct-coupled power amplifier is one op-amp (soft limit at +-31 V, 8 ohm). The **spring reverb** (driver IC1b, recovery IC4b, two springs, the REVERB pot) is inside the amp, and the Chorus module has a new **JC** mode (two voices swept in opposite directions, Rate and Depth free) with two presets. Presets Clean, Bright clean, Crunch and Lead. The preamp's gain at the drawing's test condition is 6 dB below the drawing's; the Lite mode is not fitted to this amp (it carries the JCM800's block).
- **"American Lead C+" (a dual-channel lead amp of the Mark IIC+ kind)**, transcribed from the user's drawing: V1A / V1B with a Marshall-style tone stack, the Rhythm channel, the Lead channel (V3B lead drive, V4A, lead master) and V2B / V2A with the master; the power amp is a long-tailed ECC83 inverter whose cathodes carry the feedback network, four 6L6GC (fixed bias -52 V, 39 mA idle, with bias scales worked out for EL34, KT88 and 6550), an output transformer with 8 and 4 ohm taps and the HT supply with its choke and three RC filters. Six switches: CHANNEL (Rhythm / Lead), PULL SHIFT, TREBLE SHIFT, BRIGHT VOL 1, BRIGHT LEAD and GAIN BOOST (the LDRs and pull switches of the original); knobs VOL 1, LEAD DRIVE, LEAD MASTER, BASS, MIDDLE, TREBLE, PRESENCE and MASTER. Presets Clean, Edge of breakup, Crunch and Lead. MASTER and LEAD MASTER are dividers with the wiper as the output (as a volume control), which keeps the lead channel from overdriving the power amp at any level. Not modelled: the spring reverb, the Simul-Class switch and the C++ mods; the output transformer's impedance (2.8k plate to plate) is a guess.
- **Acoustic simulator**, a new module of the rack (Effects, **ACOUSTIC SIM**): it gives an electric guitar the resonances of a flat-top box with six body modes (air, top, back, a low-mid scoop and the presence of the strings), a high-pass, two shelves and a gentle piezo-style compression. Four body types (Dreadnought, Jumbo, Parlor, Piezo), knobs BODY, TOP, AIR, SQUASH and MIX, level kept by taking off the filters' average gain, and 8 presets (`engine/include/sas/acoustic.h`).
- **"British 800 KK" (Marshall JCM800 2203KK, Kerry King signature)**: the 2203 tube preamp and four KT88 (from the user's power-PCB drawing 2203KK-60-02, calibrated to 70 mA idle), with the **Beast input stage** (drawing 2203KK-61-02) as a switch (BEAST Off / On) and two knobs, **ASSAULT** (VR8a: gain 1 to 10 of the NJM072 stage, with the two transistor-gyrator notches and the 47k / 3n3 shelf, solved as a circuit of its own on +-15 V in front of the preamp: `preamp.pre` in amp.json) and **GATE** (the THAT4301 noise gate, which cannot be a circuit: a 3:1 downward expander on the level at the detector node, threshold from -75 to -25 dBV, 4 ms attack, 50 ms release, never more than -60 dB; approximated, not measured against the real unit). With BEAST off the amp is the plain 2203 with KT88 (the Beast is not computed). The pickup and cable models still act at the tube preamp's input, after the Beast. Presets: Clean, Edge of breakup, Crunch, Lead, Beast and Beast with a tight gate (the two Beast ones are set by ear, not level-matched).
- **Six mods for the Marshall JCM800 2203**, each its own switch under the knobs (stock in the first position of every one): **MEATY** (330 pF across R3, more mids and lows), **V1B CATHODE** (R6 10k, 4.7k or 1.5k: more gain), **V2A BYPASS** (0.68u across R9: a Plexi-like midrange), **SLOPE** (R15 33k or 47k: fatter mids), **MID POT** (the middle pot 22k or 50k) and **V1A BYPASS** (C1 0.68u or 0.47u). They come from the page schematicheaven.net/mods/22032204mods.html, whose component numbers match the amp's netlist. For this an amp can now have **six switches** instead of four, and a switch can fit a different pot (`pot_totals` in a selector position). With every mod off the amp is the stock one (the level and bands are the same; the waveform differs by about -80 dB, from the rounding of the extra parts).
- **New power tubes** on the amp page, drawn from artwork (`plugin/tubes/tube.vectorcraft`): glass with its tip, mica, plates, the heater coil (always lit), the metal base and the pins. The plates glow with the plate current and the tube throws a warm halo over the panel; the glow exaggerates a change from what the tube idles at, so when the amp is pushed the tubes flare up (the plates burn white-hot) and settle back as it eases.
- **The amp and the cabinet can be removed** (the button of their unit, or right click on the tile), and put back from the **+** menu (Amp, Cabinet): the rack plays with no amp or no cabinet, a saved project without them loads without them, and in a parallel the first amp and cabinet stay in lane A while the second ones are always in lane B. Only the first noise gate stays. (A parallel needs an amp or a cabinet to build lane A around.)
- **The American Bass 300 has presets for a bass guitar**, instead of the guitar ones: Clean, Warm tube, Motown round, Deep Ultra Lo, Slap scoop, Rock grind, Overdriven and Both channels, measured with a low tone and a hotter pickup, with its Ultra Lo / Ultra Hi switches and mid frequency set for each.
- **Factory presets for every amp** (all but the NAM loader), from a **Presets** button in the amp's unit: **Clean**, **Edge of breakup**, **Crunch** and **Lead**. Each one sets the channel (jack), the knobs of the panel and the switches of the amp (the Boutique's overdrive trim, the Mod BE's SAT...), and they follow the amp that is selected. They are in each `amp.json` ("presets": a name, a jack, "params" on the panel's 0..10 scale and "selectors") and were worked out by measuring how hard the amp is driven (`sas_amp_presets`: the compression of a guitar-level sine against a very quiet one, about 1, 3, 7 and 13 dB), so the gain knob lands where the amp really clips; a preset that an amp cannot reach (a Twin does not get to a 13 dB lead) sits at the nearest end of the knob.
- **Factory presets for every module that had none**, from the **Presets** button of its unit: the noise gate (6), compressor (7), overdrive (8), phaser (8), chorus (8, with the two Juno buttons), vibe (7), tremolo (8), flanger (8), pitch shifter (8: octaves, fifths, half and whole step down...), clean boost (8) and limiter (7). Choosing one sets every knob of the module and switches it on; "Off" only switches it off and keeps the knobs (plugin/ModulePresets.h). The delay, reverb, tape echo, fuzz, wah and EQ keep their own.
- **Right click on a tile of the rack** opens a menu: **Remove** (the ends of a parallel ask about the parallel) and **Replace with**, which puts another module in the same place and lane, at its defaults and switched on. The amps, the cabinets and the first noise gate stay; a type at its limit and the module's own type are greyed out.
- The **modulation effects with a wet/dry blend inside (phaser, chorus, vibe, flanger) have a Mix knob** (`ph_mix`, `ch_mix`, `vb_mix`, `fl_mix`, 0 to 100 %). At 100 % (the default, so old projects sound the same) the effect is as it always was; below that the dry signal comes back. The tremolo has none: its depth already is the mix.
- **Spanish interface**, detected from the language of the system (any Spanish; every other language gets English), and a **Language** choice (Auto, English, Español) in the Presets menu. It is saved in the user's application data folder (`Schematic Amp Sim/language.txt`, shared by the plugin and the standalone) and applies the next time the editor window is opened. Texts come from `plugin/I18nEs.cpp` through `tr("...")`: a text with no translation shows in English. Parameter names (what the host shows for automation) and the names of the amps and cabinets stay as they are.
- A **modular rack**. Everything but the tuner is now a module of an ordered rack: the amp (with its condition and speaker dynamics), the cabinet, noise gate, compressor, overdrive, NAM pedal, phaser, chorus, vibe, pitch, EQ, delay and reverb. The noise gate, the amp and the cabinet are always there (they cannot be removed; the gate and the cabinet can be switched off from their tile, the cabinet giving the amp's raw signal); a **+** button adds the rest (up to twelve besides the amp; several of most kinds, so two overdrives or two reverbs), they are reordered by dragging or with the arrows (a reverb before the amp, a delay in front of the cabinet), and removed with the x. Reordering fades the output for a few milliseconds so nothing clicks, and a module keeps its state (a reverb its tail) when it moves. Each unit shows its CPU use; a mono module after a stereo one says it sums to mono.
- **Tremolo** and **Flanger** (Modulation menu of the rack), up to two of each. The tremolo has sine, triangle and rounded-square waves, rate, depth and a stereo auto-pan; the flanger a swept short delay with rate, depth, manual (centre of the sweep) and feedback from -90 to +90 %, with a quarter-cycle offset between the ears on a stereo pair. Both work on a mono or a stereo signal, wherever the player puts them (host ids `tr_*`, `fl_*`).
- Every modulation module (phaser, chorus, vibe, tremolo, flanger) has a **Channels: Mono / Stereo** choice (`*_stereo`). Mono (the default, so old projects sound the same) gives both channels of a stereo pair the same modulation; Stereo puts the right channel's sweep out of step with the left's (half a cycle in the phaser, vibe and tremolo, which then pans; a quarter in the flanger; the chorus CE-2 sweeps the other way; the Juno chorus is always two voices). It only has something to work on where the signal is stereo, that is after the cabinet.
- The **delay** and the **reverb** have the same Channels choice (`dly_stereo`, `pl_stereo`). Delay: Mono (the default, as it always was) or Stereo, where the left repeats come at three quarters of the time of the right ones, so the two ears hear different echoes (ping-pong is stereo already). Reverb: Stereo (the default, as it always was) or Mono, the same tail on both sides.
- **Wah** (Effects menu), up to two. A resonant band-pass that sweeps between a **Heel** and a **Toe** frequency (both free), with Q, mix, level and a Mono/Stereo choice. What moves it is your choice: **Pedal** (a position you can automate or map to a controller in the host), **Envelope** (an auto-wah that follows how hard you play, with sensitivity, attack, release and Up/Down) or **LFO** (it rocks by itself, with a rate). The panel shows only the controls of the chosen mode. The wah has 10 factory **presets** (Classic pedal, Vox style, Throaty lead, Cocked wah, Subtle, Funk quack, Envelope down, Snappy auto-wah, Slow sweep, Stereo swirl; choosing one switches it on, "Off" switches it off). A new wah goes in front of the compressor and overdrive that sit next to the amp (`wah_*` host ids).
- **Fuzz** (Effects menu), up to two, in DSP at twice the host rate. Three types: **Face** (an asymmetric, furry soft clip; Bias starves it until it gates and sputters), **Muff** (two clipping stages and a tone stack with the mid scoop in the middle of the knob) and **Octave** (a rectified signal that adds an octave above). Fuzz, Tone, Bias, Mix (the dry guitar blended in, kept in phase with the oversampling latency) and Level, and 8 factory presets (`fz_*` host ids). Like the Tube Screamer it is mono, loads the guitar in front of the amp and goes in front of the compressor and overdrive next to the amp.
- **Clean Boost** and **Limiter** (Effects menu), up to two of each, on a mono or stereo signal. The boost is a plain gain of 0 to 30 dB with a tone tilt around 800 Hz (up to 6 dB either way) and an optional soft clip for when it runs out of headroom. The limiter is a brick wall with a 1 ms lookahead (reported to the host as latency): Gain pushes the signal in, Ceiling is the loudest it can ever be, Release how fast the gain comes back; on a stereo pair both channels get the same gain. A new boost goes in front of the amp, a new limiter at the end of the chain (`bst_*`, `lim_*` host ids).
- **Tape Echo** (Space menu), up to two: a loop of tape with three playback heads at 1, 2 and 3 times the Time, and a Heads choice for any of the seven combinations. Intensity is the feedback (past 100 % the repeats swell and self-oscillate, bounded by the tape saturation), Tone is how many highs the loop carries (each repeat is darker and thinner), Saturation drives the record head, Wow and Flutter wobble the tape speed so the repeats bend in pitch, and changing Time glides the pitch like a real tape. Mono or Stereo (the heads spread left, middle, right) and 7 factory presets (`tape_*` host ids). It rings out when switched off.
- The **equaliser has eight bands** instead of five: three more bells (`eq6_*` to `eq8_*`, at 500 Hz, 2 kHz and 10 kHz, each with its own frequency range, gain and Q). The first five are as they always were (band 1 the low shelf, band 5 the high shelf), so old projects and presets sound the same; the new ones start flat, and the factory presets leave them flat.
- The extra instances have their own host parameters (`ts_drive__2`, `pl_decay__2`...); the first instance of each kind keeps the ids it always had, so automation keeps working.

### Changed
- **Choosing another amp puts that amp's own power tubes back**: a tube type picked for one amp (6L6, EL34...) no longer follows the player to the next amp; the new amp starts with its stock tubes. It applies to amp A (the list) and to amp B (its selector).
- The **cabinet keeps a stereo pair**: when a stereo signal reaches it (a stereo effect in front of it), each channel goes through the same filter, NAM model or impulse response and it stays stereo; before, the pair was summed to mono first. With two impulses the blend applies to both channels (the A/B width, which makes stereo out of a mono signal, only works on a mono one). A mono signal behaves as it always did.
- A module that is added or put in the place of another **starts switched on**; before, a new pedal or effect started off.
- **Preamps split into small circuits** for the American Bass 300 (SVT), the American Lead 100 and the British Mod 100: the same circuits, solved in three or four parts instead of one, which takes the CPU of those amps down by a third to a half (for instance the Bass 300 on a hot lead from 48 % to 35 % of a core, the Lead 100 from 65 % to 32 %). The level and tone match the whole circuit within half a dB; the Bass 300's second channel loses about 3 dB at 110 Hz, because the two channels no longer share a supply filter.
- **Parallel paths**: the + menu has **Parallel**, which adds a splitter and a mixer. The signal splits into lane A (the first amp and cabinet) and lane B, which starts empty (a dry path): the player decides what goes in it, from the **Lane B** submenu of the + menu — a second amp with its own cabinet, or just a reverb, a delay, a pedal... Either lane can hold any module, and they are mixed with a level, a pan and a phase switch per lane; the mixer lines the two lanes up in time (their latencies are compared and the earlier one is delayed), and a lane whose amp is still being built plays nothing and comes in smoothly. A module before the splitter or after the mixer is shared; one inside a lane is for that amp only (a compressor and an overdrive right next to an amp still run inside it). The second amp has its own parameters (`volume__2`...), circuits, condition and impulse responses; its cabinet has no NAM mode (it plays the filter) and a NAM Loader amp in lane B plays the model of the first one. The header shows the total CPU of the rack. Projects without a parallel are unchanged.
- A new instance opens in the **Efficient** quality (projects keep the quality they were saved with).
- The standalone window can be maximised, and the editor shows the product icon.
- The editor has two pages, **Rack** and **Tuner**; the Amp, Cabinet, Effects, Pitch, EQ, Mod and Space pages are units of the rack. The **amp condition** (tubes, bias, noise, wear, unit) and the speaker dynamics are part of the amp unit.
- A compressor and an overdrive right in front of the amp (in that order) still run inside the amp's own front end, so the sound of existing projects does not change.
- The Pre / Post switches of the phaser, chorus and vibe are gone: they are positions in the rack (the old values are read once to place the modules of an old project).

### Migrated
- Projects and presets saved before the rack (without a `<RACK>` node) open as they were: the rack is built from the old parameters, with the same order, and renders the same sound (checked against reference renders). It holds the modules that were on or had their knobs touched, as bypassed modules if they were off, plus the gate, the amp and the cabinet; a switch that an old project automated but that was untouched in the saved state has no module to act on until you add one.

### Known limitations (new in this version)
- The new amps were transcribed from the users' drawings and measured, not compared with the real amplifiers; their output levels were set by measurement and the tone is not tuned by ear.
- The American Lead C+ has no reverb, no Simul-Class switch and no mods of the C++ kind; the American Clean 120's power amplifier is one op-amp (not the discrete stage); the American 5153's feedback, presence and resonance board is assumed.
- The Klon-style drive has no charge pump (fixed rails) and an assumed germanium diode model; the spring reverb's tail does not ring out when the module is switched off.
- The automated tests were not run for this version.

- The **Level match** switch is gone: the compensation between the jacks and modes of an amp is always on (it was on by default). Old projects load as before.

### Added
- A **NAM pedal** on the Effects page: a Neural Amp Modeler model of an overdrive, boost or fuzz pedal in front of the amp (after the Mod page's pre-amp effects), with an On switch, Drive and Level in dB and its own Load... button. It works with every amp, the NAM Loader included, costs nothing while off, and the model is kept inside the project state.
- A **NAM cabinet**: a fourth cabinet mode, "NAM", that runs a Neural Amp Modeler model of a speaker cabinet (with or without the microphone) after the amp and the speaker, in place of the impulse responses and the filter. It works with every amp, the NAM Loader included; the Cabinet page has its own Load NAM... button and name. The model is kept inside the project state, and with the mode on but no model loaded the plugin falls back to the cabinet filter.
- Two more amps: the **American Deluxe** (a 22 W blackface combo with two 6V6GT, reverb and vibrato, from the '65 Deluxe Reverb reissue service manual) and the **American Boutique** (a 100 W overdrive head with a Clean / Overdrive switch, a Rock / Jazz tone switch and Deep and Bright switches, from a 1984 hand-built head's schematic). The **American Lead 100** is a 100 W two-channel super-lead head (Clean / Crunch and Overdrive channels, from a 1996 schematic with corrections) with four 6L6GC. The **British Mod 100** is a 100 W modded-British head with Clean, BE and HBE modes, from its published schematic. All four are solved circuits like the others; their Lite and Eco models are fitted. The reading of the Boutique's tone switches is documented in its netlist.
- A **NAM Loader** amp (the last of the list): it runs a Neural Amp Modeler model (`.nam` file) in place of the circuits, with DRIVE into the model, a three-band tone control (BASS, MIDDLE, TREBLE) after it and LEVEL out of it, a Load model... button and the same pedals, noise gate, cabinet, EQ and effects as the other amps. The model's bytes are saved inside the project, so it opens on another machine with its model; the model is brought to a common loudness. It runs at the host rate; a model trained at another rate is flagged as off-pitch. Uses the Neural Amp Modeler Core (MIT), fetched by CMake into `third_party/` and never copied into the repository.
- A fifth quality, **Efficient (2x/1x)**: the full circuits with the preamp at twice the host rate and the power amp at the host rate (a half-band filter between them). The power amp is 60 to 80 % of the cost of Normal; this uses 5 to 25 % less CPU on soft playing and about half on a loud, dense signal, with the same loudness and close tone, but a little more aliasing when the amp is driven hard and a tone difference of a few tenths of a dB. Normal stays as it was. Measured in `docs/PLAN_CPU.md`.
- Faster solving of the output stage in Normal and High when it is driven hard (the SVT and the Hiwatt: up to ~25 % less CPU at full gain, with the same sound), and diagnostic tools (`sas_compare`, `sas_diag_newton`, `sas_compare_rate`, `sas_bench` per mode).
- A Space page with a delay and a reverb at the end of the chain (after the cabinet and the EQ). The delay: up to 1.5 s, feedback, mix, a tone control in the repeats, ping-pong, an "analog" mode (darker, a little saturation and
  a slow wobble), tempo sync to the host (1/4, dotted 1/8, 1/8, 1/16) and a Tap button. The reverb: a plate or, with Hall, a larger room, with decay, pre-delay, tone, low cut, mix and modulation. Both let their tails ring out when switched off.
  The reverb has a Shimmer knob: a copy of its tail shifted up an octave (or an octave and a fifth) is fed back into it, so the tail climbs on every pass. Each effect has its own list of factory presets (eight for the delay, ten for the reverb, two of them shimmer) that only change that effect.
- A Mod page with a phaser, a chorus and a vibe, each with a Pre amp / Post amp switch (in front of the amp, or after the cabinet and the EQ). The phaser: four all-pass stages, the single Speed knob of the classic pedal and an Intense switch for the feedback version. The chorus has two modes in one effect: CE-2 (one voice, Rate and Depth) and Juno (two voices, one in each ear, with the fixed buttons I, II and I+II). The vibe has four unlike all-pass stages swept by a lamp-and-photocell model (Speed, Intensity, and a Chorus / Vibrato switch); it sits in front of the amp by default. All three are the behaviour of the originals in DSP, not their solved circuits (the solver has no JFETs, bucket-brigade chips or photocells).
- The EQ page shows a silhouette of what is sounding behind the curve: the output spectrum in a sixth of an octave steps, tilted +4.5 dB per octave so the highs do not disappear, with a fast rise and a slow fall.
- Eight cabinet impulse responses (4x12 OS Mix 1 to 3, Green Mix 1 to 3, Org Mix 1 and Fried Mix 1) built into the plugin: the Load buttons of the Cabinet page open a menu with them and "From file...". A new instance has Mix 1 loaded in slot A.
- A new amp, American Bass 300, from the preamp and power amp drawings of a 1972 tube bass head with the Ultra Hi and Ultra Lo switches: two channels, the bass select network, a bass / treble stack per channel,
  the mid-range network with its toroidal inductor, six 6550. The switches are a new kind of control: an amp can now declare up to four "selectors" (amp.json `preamp.selectors`), shown as drop-downs under its
  knobs and saved as parameters. Several readings of the drawing are interpretations (see the comments in `amps/svt_bass300/*.net`): the bass-select and channel 2 Ultra Lo networks, the exact taps of the toroid,
  the cathode-follower drivers, the supply decoupling (a second filter stage was added: with the drawing's single filter this model motorboats). The 6550 model was fitted at much lower plate voltages than the
  660 V here. The Lite mode does not follow the full circuit well on this amp (it cannot reproduce the speaker and transformer shape of a feedback-free amp).
- Ten guitar presets for the EQ alone (the Presets button on the EQ page; they only set the EQ): Clean sparkle, Warm blues, Tight rhythm, Lead cut-through, Smooth (de-fizz), Metal scoop, Jazz warm, Country twang, Classic rock crunch and Mix-ready, plus Flat to reset.

## [0.2.0-beta] - 2026-10-28

### Added
- A harmonic exciter ("maximizer") on the EQ page, after the EQ: a BASS amount adds harmonics of the lowest band (under 140 Hz) and a TREBLE amount adds harmonics of the
  highest (over 3.5 kHz), each with a gentle shelf. The harmonics follow the band's own level, so they are the same percentage at any playing level. Both at 0 is bit-exact bypass.
  It does not do the time alignment of the three bands that a hardware maximizer does.
- A post equaliser on its own EQ page: five bands (low shelf, three bells, high shelf) plus a high-pass and a low-pass, after the cabinet and the double tracking, with a curve to drag
  (position = frequency and gain, wheel = Q, double click = gain 0). Flat means bypassed and bit-exact; the parameters glide over 15 ms.
- The Orange-style amp is now called British Or.
- A pitch shifter on its own Pitch page: -12 to +12 semitones and -50 to +50 cents, in front of the whole chain (the tuner still listens to the raw input). It is the
  granular two-head shifter of the earlier Rust octave plugin, with a 20 ms grain; it adds about 12 ms of delay, reported to the host, only while a shift is set.
- A new amp, British Or, from a 1970s 120 W head schematic (preamp, output amp and power supply drawings): the Orange tone circuit, a six-position FAC
  switch (shown as the input list), four EL34 and 15k feedback with a BOOST control. Which EL34 pair drives which end of the transformer is not drawn;
  it is chosen so the feedback is negative. The jack levels of the FAC positions are only partly matched (the amp is saturated). See `amps/orange_or120/*.net`.
- The amp list is scrollable and has a search box above it.
- A new amp, British Hi-Fi, from a 100 W high-headroom head owner's manual (preamp issue 4, output stage, power supply): brilliant and normal channels, passive
  tone stack with master volume, four EL34 and a silicon bridge supply. The printed -38 V bias idles 53 mA per tube with this tube model, so the source
  is set for -43 V (about 38 mA). The plate voltages printed on the preamp are 40 to 75 V lower than the model's; the 100 pF between the preamp's
  last plate and the presence pot is left out (see the comments in `amps/hiwatt_dr103/*.net`).
- A new amp, American Bass 50, from the 5F6-A tweed bass head schematic (Kuehnel's redrawing): bright and normal channels with #1 and #2 inputs,
  two 6L6GC, tube rectifier and choke. The bias source and the rectifier are tuned, not read, because the drawing prints no idle current.
- A new amp, American Triple Stack, from a three-channel solo head schematic (preliminary drawing): channel 1 Clean / Pushed, channels 2 and 3 with
  Raw / Vintage / Modern voicings, four 6L6GC. The relays, the effects loop and the per-channel presence feedback are not modelled; the voicings
  are interpretations of the drawing (see the comments in `amps/triplestack_3ch/*.net`).
- A macOS build (VST3 and Audio Unit, one universal binary for Apple Silicon and Intel, macOS 11 or later), packaged by `package-macos.sh`.
  Not tried on a real Mac yet and not notarized.
- GitHub Actions: `ci.yml` builds and tests on Windows and macOS on every push; `release.yml` builds both and creates a draft release when a
  `v*` tag is pushed.

### Changed
- The output transformers have winding capacitance (150 pF across the primary) and core loss (about 21 times the plate-to-plate impedance), set per amp in
  `transformer_parasitics`: a roll-off and a resonance in the treble. The core loss also damps the resonance: without it the 120 W high-gain amp rings at rest
  with its feedback loop, and 300 pF rings even with it.
- The power amps drive a reactive speaker load (voice-coil resistance and inductance, cone resonance) instead of a plain resistor: the output now has a bass bump, a mid dip
  and a rising top like a tube amp into a real speaker. The Lite fits and the output levels of every amp were redone. Lite cannot reproduce the bump and the dip
  (worst fit error about 9 dB on the small amps). Each amp can opt out with `speaker_load.enabled = false`.
- The input jack selector has 8 positions (it had 4); saved projects keep their selection. Amps list order is unchanged, the new amp is last.
- Strict floating point (`-ffp-contract=off`) on clang and gcc, as `/fp:precise` on MSVC.

## [0.1.0-beta] - 2026-10-27

The first public release: a Windows VST3 with seven circuit-level amplifiers.

### Amps
- Seven amps, each a netlist transcribed from a schematic and solved as a nonlinear circuit in real time: British 800, British Plexi,
  SLSH 100W, American Twin (optical vibrato and spring reverb), American 5050, British Class A and American Tweed.
- Quality modes Normal (2x), High (4x), Lite and Eco, with level matching across amps, jacks and modes.
- Output transformer core saturation, tube and solid-state rectifier supplies with sag, a choke on the Class A amp.
- Data-driven: a new amp is a folder with `amp.json` and its netlists; nothing is recompiled except to embed them.

### Realism
- Output tube choice (EL34, 6L6GC, 6550 where the amp is fixed-bias) re-biased to the same share of the tube's rating, a bias control with
  the plate current read from the circuit, noise and hum that depend on the pickup, wear, and a "unit" number that draws component tolerances.
- Guitar input: single coil, humbucker or P-90 and cable capacitance in front of every amp.
- Glowing power tubes in the editor that follow the plate current.

### Cabinet and stereo
- Cabinet off, a simple filter, or two impulse responses (A and B, each with its response curve) with an equal-power blend; stored in the project.
- Speaker dynamics between the amp and the cabinet: cone excursion, voice-coil heating and break-up, with an Intense mode.
- Fake stereo: impulse A on the left and B on the right (Width), automatic double tracking (Double and Time). A mono track can feed the
  stereo output.

### Effects and tools
- Compressor pedal solved as a circuit from the Keeley Compressor Plus schematic (an OTA gain cell, a transistor envelope detector and the
  feedback that closes the loop), a Tube Screamer style overdrive, a noise gate.
- Chromatic tuner with a needle meter, a reference pitch of 430 to 450 Hz and an option to mute the output.

### Editor
- A list of amps on the left, four pages (Amp, Cabinet, Effects, Tuner), colours and knobs that change with each amp, level meters.

### Known limitations
- Windows (VST3) only. AU and AAX are planned.
- The tube, transformer, rectifier, pickup and noise values are typical figures, not measurements of a particular amp; the schematics
  print none. They are documented next to each netlist.
- The Lite and Eco modes use the stock amp: the output tube choice, wear and unit act in Normal and High only.
- The tuner detects from 60 Hz to about 1.4 kHz.
- While the compressor or the overdrive is on, the pickup and cable of the Guitar Input panel have no effect.
- The saved-project format may still change between 0.x versions.
