## [0.8.0-beta] - 2026-11-22

### Added
- **Four new amps**, each transcribed from its schematic:
  - **British Silver** (Silver Jubilee, 100 W, four EL34): V1a / V1b into a Rhythm / Lead relay (selector CHANNEL), the LED clipping network and LEAD MASTER in Lead, a RHYTHM CLIP selector (the diodes on the pull switch of the gain pot), the tone stack with a two-section BASS, the OUTPUT MASTER and the power amp with its presence network. The effects loop is bypassed.
  - **American Class A** (a 30 W Class A head, four EL84): Channel 1 (two ECC83 in parallel, volume with bright cap, the Top Boost network) and Channel 2 (an EF86, stood in for by an ECC83, with the six-position TONE switch), jacks Channel 1 / Channel 2 / Both; MASTER is a pot between the two phases after the inverter (a power knob with a complement) and CUT is a cap and a pot between them. Built from an image of the schematic with some assumptions that are written in the netlist.
  - **American Lead V** (a three-channel 90 W head): CH1 (Clean, Fat, Tweed), CH2 (Edge, Crunch, Mark I) and CH3 (IIC+, IV, Extreme), each with its own tone stack, the CH3 feedback around its gain stages, V3A / V6A, the per-channel masters and the loop's V6B; a five-band graphic EQ drawn as vertical faders. The power amp is the Lead C+'s.
  - **German Quad** (a four-channel 100 W head): CH1 Clean, CH2 Crunch, CH3 Lead 1 and CH4 Lead 2 through the shared V2A / V2B and their own stacks, BRIGHT switches on two channels, four EL34 with the voltages of the real amp. DEEP, the loops, MIX and the inserts are not modelled.
- **Tuner: tunings and strings.** A TUNING selector with 14 tunings besides the chromatic mode: guitar standard, half step down, whole step down, drop D, drop C, double drop D, DADGAD, open G, D and E, 7 strings (standard and drop A) and bass with 4 or 5 strings. The strings are drawn, the one being played lights up, the ones that were in tune are ticked off, the cents are measured from the string and the display says "tune up" or "tune down". The detector now goes down to the lowest string of the tuning (about 25 Hz for a 5-string bass, instead of 60 Hz).
- **Tuner: faster and easier to read.** The analysis window follows the lowest note (about 45 ms for a standard guitar instead of 128 ms), the display is updated 15 times a second, and the last note stays on the screen, dimmed, for 0.6 s when the string fades. An arc dial (+-50 cents) with the flat side in blue, the sharp side in red and the in-tune zone (+-5 cents) in green, a strip with the pitch of the last 8 seconds, an input level bar with the sensitivity marked and a SENS dB knob (`tuner_sens`, -75 to -35 dB) for guitars or interfaces with a weak signal.
- **More EQ presets:** six for the parametric EQ (Modern metal, Stoner doom, Funk chops, Ambient shimmer, Brighten dark amp, Quiet loudness: 17 in all) and six for the ten-band graphic EQ (Modern djent, Heavy low end, Funk chops, Acoustic body, Vintage radio, Shimmer air: 14 in all).
- **Graphic EQ faders:** the five-band graphic EQ of the Lead C+ and the Lead V is drawn as five vertical faders with a scale, like the real amps (`"style": "slider"` on an entry of `ui`).
- `sas_ab_check` (compares two amps' preamps: level, third-octave bands, waveform, silence and the swing of each node), `sas_noise_check` (the noise and the silence of the whole chain, per amp and per setting) and `SAS_SEL` in `sas_bench_chain`.

### Changed
- **New look:** the knobs have an outer ring that shows the value (with a glow) and no tick marks, the panels are recessed with softer corners, and the combo boxes, buttons and switches are recessed too. The NAM Loader's controls are in two rows by band, with a block for each group.
- **American 5153:** the presence and resonance networks are the ones of the real EVH 5150 III power amp (the resonance pot is a 1M log from the 8 ohm tap into the feedback loop, the presence is a 10k rheostat with 0.136u to ground); the amp sounded shrill with the assumed values. The Lite fit was made again.
- **Preamps split in stages** to use less CPU: American Lead V (each channel in two or three stages, only the one you hear is solved), German Quad (each channel before its shared V2A) and American Class A (channel 1 up to its cathode follower, channel 2 on its own). Checked against the unsplit circuits with `sas_ab_check`: the level within 0.1 dB and the bands within 2.5 dB; the gain in CPU is 4 to 14 % in Normal and up to 30 % in Lite. (The stages are cut where a grid does not conduct and the Miller capacitance is kept.)
- `power.knobs` accept a `taper` and a `complement` (the other section of a pot that is in a divider), and there are two more diode models (`SIGNAL4`, `SIGNAL5`: four and five junctions in series).

### Fixed
- **The NAM Loader did not play the model you loaded** (the loader is the 33rd amp and the chain key only held 32): it played the first amp of the list whatever the model. The key now holds 128 amps and the plug-in checks it, and the component edits are tracked for every amp.
- **American Lead V, channel 3: self-oscillation.** With no input the channel produced a 100 Hz motorboating (the HT of the six stages was one single filter and the power amp's sag came back through it). The supply now has the filters of the real amp (C: 2.55k x 33u, E: 1k x 33u, D: 47k x 10u) and the preamp's solver tolerance is 2e-8: the silence is -114 dBFS (Eco) and -88 dBFS (Normal). The two capacitors at V3A's grid (47p and 500p) were missing.
- **British Silver: Lead had the gain of Rhythm.** The relay's common contact (R6, R7, R8) was not connected to ground in Lead and the clipping network's bottom was not on ground. Lead now has about 16 dB more gain than Rhythm and clips through its LEDs.
- **The tuner's REF Hz knob** (the reference pitch, 430 to 450 Hz) was never added to the editor, so it could not be changed.
- A selector's first position must match the values of the netlist (it is not applied again at start-up): the Jubilee's Rhythm and the Class A's tone had a different value.

## [0.7.0-beta] - 2026-11-19

### Added
- **NAM loader: advanced parameters.** The loader amp now has GAIN, an EQ with BASS, MIDDLE and TREBLE, each with its own frequency (HZ knobs; the middle also has Q), a POST EQ switch that puts the three bands after the model (on, as before) or in front of it (after the gain), PRESENCE (a high shelf near 4.5 kHz), DEPTH (a bell near 80 Hz) and VOLUME. The new parameters (`nam_bass_hz`, `nam_mid_hz`, `nam_mid_q`, `nam_treble_hz`, `nam_eq_post`, and the second amp's) default to what the loader did before, so saved projects sound the same. The editor draws an on/off parameter of an amp's `ui` as a switch.

### Changed
- **The NAM models run about twice as fast on Windows**: the NAM core (Eigen) is compiled with AVX2 and FMA (MSVC `/arch:AVX2`), which every x86 CPU since 2013-2015 has. A standard WaveNet model went from 386 % to 183 % of a core in the benchmark with only this change.

## [0.6.0-beta] - 2026-11-18

### Added
- **AAX build (Pro Tools), off by default**: `cmake -DSAS_AAX_SDK_DIR=<path to Avid's AAX SDK>` adds the AAX format (Windows and macOS). The SDK is not redistributable, so it is not fetched and the CI does not build AAX; Pro Tools also needs the plug-in signed with Avid's PACE tools. No AAX package is published yet and it has not been tried in Pro Tools (see docs/RELEASING.md).
- **Circuit page:** a third tab beside Rack and Tuner shows the amp's signal path (guitar, the stages named in `amp.json` "probes", speaker) with a live level meter for each: the bar is the level now, the line the recent peak, and the stage turns red when it reaches its limit. The British 800 has four stages of its own (V1, V2, TONE, MASTER) and its schematic, drawn in VectorCraft (`amps/<id>/face/circuit.svg`): the wire of each stage takes the colour of its level, the tubes glow with the signal the pots are the amp's knobs (drag them, wheel, double-click for the default), a second drawing shows the power amp (phase inverter, output tubes, transformer and the presence pot; its stages are measured too in the Normal, High and Efficient qualities) the components carry their values in small type (read from the netlists for the generated drawings), and the meters sit in a strip below. The other amps that have tubes get a schematic of the same look, generated by `tools/gen_circuits.py` from their netlists (one tube per stage of the preamp, the pots of the panel, the power amp with its feedback pots); the American Tweed (5E3) has its real circuit drawn by `tools/circuits_hand.py`, and so do the British Plexi, British 45 and Bassman 5F6-A (their bright and normal channels, V2 and cathode follower, and the bass-middle-treble stack, with their netlist values; the power amp keeps the generated drawing). The high-gain amps (5153, Super Lead, Lead C+, Triple Stack, Dual 100, JCM900, HighGain 120, Trio, Mod BE, 800 KK) are drawn from their netlists as a chain: the tubes in signal order and, between them, the real networks (series path and what hangs off it, with the pots as live knobs); a long chain wraps into rows. Any drawing can be dragged along, zoomed with the wheel and shown whole or at a readable size with a double-click. The drawings also show the clipping diodes (they glow with the signal that reaches them), the switches that modes and channels set (open or closed as the amp is set: they follow the jack and the selectors) and a VALUES button that hides or shows the components' values. Amps with several channels (the 5153, Super Lead, Mod BE, German Trio and Ultra, SVT) show one drawing per channel, with its own tubes and pots and a tab for each (`circuit_channels` in `amp.json`); the page follows the channel the amp is set to. The Orange OR120, the Hiwatt DR103, the Vox AC30 and the three Fenders (Deluxe Reverb, Twin Reverb and the Bassbreaker 18/30) have theirs too. The Tweed (both channels with their volume pots, the tone and brightness network, the cathodyne phase inverter and the 6V6 pair), with the values of its netlist; the Modern Bass 800 (GK) and the American Clean 120 (Jazz) have theirs too: with no tubes, their op-amps are drawn as triangles with their feedback network (the stages that take the signal on the inverting input have the + input tied to ground), the same real networks between stages, a transistor power amp drawn as one op-amp with its feedback, and meters calibrated on each op-amp's output; only the NAM loader (a neural network, no circuit) keeps the blocks.
- **Edit the components of the circuit:** in the drawings of the Circuit page, the value of a resistor or capacitor can be changed with the wheel or by dragging it up and down (one step of the E12 series for each notch or 9 pixels; a value that is not in the series is passed through on the way back to it), and a double-click puts it back. The changed values show in the accent colour and the RESET button next to VALUES puts them all back. The edit is given to the amp a moment after the last step: the amp's circuits are built again off the audio thread, as when a tube or the wear is chosen, with the factor applied to the part in the netlist (0.1 to 10 times its value; the pots, switches, the guitar's source and the power amp are not edited). The edits are kept for each amp and saved with the project (`PARTS` in the state). The generated drawings, the Plexi, British 45, Bassman, Orange, Hiwatt, AC30 and the three Fenders carry the netlist names of their values (`valL_<n>_<text>@R:R12`), so those can be edited; the British 800, the Tweed and the SVT show their values but are not editable yet.
- **Tips and tubes on the Circuit page:** with the mouse on the value of a component, a box says what it is (plate load, cathode resistor, bypass, coupling capacitor, grid leak, tone capacitor...) and what a larger or a smaller value does to the sound; the part's role is read from where it sits in the netlists, so it works in every drawing. The preamp triodes can be swapped too: a click or the wheel on a tube steps through 12AX7, 12AT7 and 12AY7 (a double-click goes back to the drawn one), the new model is written in the accent colour, a tip tells what each model does (and that a cathode follower changes little), and the amp is rebuilt like for the values; RESET brings back both. The power amp's drawing has the same: the phase inverter's tube ("PI") can be swapped too (the whole inverter changes model in the power netlist), and the output tubes show a tip about the tube the amp is set to (EL34, 6L6GC, 6550, 6V6GT, EL84), whose name is also written under them; they are still chosen with the POWER TUBES selector. The generated power drawings now also show, under the tubes, three boxes with the parts that decide the power amp's character, read from its netlist (up to eight each): PHASE INVERTER (plate loads, tail, grid leaks, couplings), FEEDBACK (the part of the loop around the presence pot) and SUPPLY AND BIAS (the supply resistor and filters, the screens, the output grids and the bias); each part has its value and a tip (phase inverter load or tail, negative feedback, supply or bias resistor...). They are not edited: the bias and the supply are calibrated together. The choice is kept with the plug-in state (`T:<tube>` in the same part edits; a name such as V1 covers V1A and V1B). In Spanish; the other languages show the tips in English.
- **Efficient SD-1:** the Super OverDrive pedal of the Overdrive module is now solved analytically (`sas/sd1_fast.h`), as the Tube Screamer is: the three clipping diodes of its first op-amp are one Newton equation and every other stage is a filter from the schematic's values (the input network, the gain stage's leg and feedback, the low pass and tone stage as one second order section, the level control). It costs about 2.5 % of a core at 48 kHz instead of 80 % or more of the nodal solver, and matches it within 1 dB in the level of the note and within a few dB in its harmonics for playing-size signals (`DriveSolve::Circuit` keeps the solver as the reference; the test compares them). The other pedals are still solved as circuits.
- **Efficient RAT:** the RAT style distortion of the Overdrive module is solved analytically too (`sas/rat_fast.h`): its linear half (the input network and the LM308 gain stage, with the op-amp's finite gain-bandwidth that limits the highs at full distortion) is one transfer function built from the netlist's values and run as a cascade of sections (`sas/rational.h`: the same trapezoidal response as the solver, from the circuit's H(s)), and the clipper (R6, C7, the two diodes and the FILTER on the same node) is one Newton equation per sample. About 3.8 % of a core at 48 kHz instead of 11.5 %, within 0.15 dB of the circuit in the note and within about 1 dB in the harmonics for playing-size signals. The Blues Driver, the DS-1, the Klon and the compressor are still solved as circuits.
- **Efficient DS-1:** the DS-1 style distortion (the Distortion module) is solved by hand too (`sas/ds1_fast.h`): the first transistor is a linear follower, the booster is a full Ebers-Moll transistor with its emitter resistor, its 470k / 250p feedback and the input network (a 3 x 3 Newton equation per sample), the TA7136 is the op-amp as the circuit has it (its internal node, the output limit and the DIST network, so the saturation winds up and recovers as the circuit's does), and the clipper has the tone network and the LEVEL pot on its node (a 4 x 4 matrix inverted when a knob moves, plus the diodes' Newton equation). It also carries what the solver's op-amp does to the bias node (its output stage current returns through the 1 ohm bias source, so the bias moves by about 1 % of the output and the output gets up to 2.5 dB more at full distortion). About 11 % of a core at 48 kHz instead of 21 %, within 0.3 dB of the circuit in the note and within 0.6 dB in the third harmonic. The Blues Driver, the Klon and the compressor are still solved as circuits.
- **Efficient Blues Driver:** the BD-2 style overdrive is solved by hand as well (`sas/bd2_fast.h`), from small linear networks (`sas/trapnet.h`: resistors and capacitors with the trapezoidal rule and a matrix inverted when a pot moves) and the three nonlinear things in its path: each op-amp with its feedback network as the circuit has it (the internal node, the output limit and the output resistance, one Newton equation per sample; the second gain stage together with the tone, LEVEL and the third stage's input, which its 2.2k output resistance feels), and the four-diode clipper between the gain stages, whose current is taken out of one node of the network around it. About a quarter of the circuit's cost, within 0.3 dB of it in the note and within about 3 dB in the third harmonic where that is audible. Only the Klon and the Keeley compressor are left as circuits.
- **The Studio Compressor is a model now:** the first model of the Compressor module (the Keeley circuit) no longer runs the nodal solver (12 % of a core) but a model of its behaviour (`sas/keeley_comp.h`, about 0.3 %), fitted to measurements of the circuit: the wet path holds its output at about -19 dBV whatever the input (the OTA's gain from +2 dB to +27 dB with Sustain, then a ratio of 12:1 to 25:1), the gain is proportional to the envelope capacitor's voltage so it comes back after a loud note as 1 - (1 - v0) exp(-t / tau) with tau 1.6 s (0.1 s with the fast release), the wet path has a high pass near 165 Hz, a low pass near 8 kHz and the Tone shelf (up to +7.8 dB), the dry path a high pass near 68 Hz, Blend and Level follow the pots' laws, and the output limits softly at 3.3 V. Against the circuit: the static curve within 0.3 dB, the recovery within 0.1 dB, the frequency response within 1.2 dB, Blend within 0.3 dB. It differs in the first milliseconds of a loud note (the circuit lets a few cycles through before its detector acts) and it does not look at the guitar's impedance. The circuit stays in `sas/compressor.h` as the reference; the legacy golden render `rack_legacy_comp_ts` was made again. The Klon is still solved as a circuit (an analytic version matched it to 0.2 dB but cost the same).
- **SAG control:** next to BIAS in the amp's condition panel (and in the second amp's), a knob for how soft the supply is: 5 is as drawn, lower values stiffen the rectifier's resistance down to a third and higher ones soften it up to three times, so the B+ gives way more under a loud signal (in the Lite and Eco modes it scales the sag of the preamp rail). It moves with the amp's presets and is kept in the plug-in state.
- **An amp's own presets:** the Presets button of the amp module lists, after the factory presets, the ones the user saved for that amp, with "Save the current settings as a preset..." and "Delete a preset". A preset keeps the amp's knobs, jack and switches, the condition (power tubes, bias, sag, wear, unit) and the edits of its circuit (the values of the components and the models of the tubes); loading one without edits clears the ones that were there. They are small files under `Presets/Amps/<amp id>/` in the application data folder.
- **Small windows:** the amp's name at the top is no longer cut by the level meters: in a narrow window the IN and OUT bars shrink until the name fits, and a very long name is squeezed.
- **Interface:** the amp list has a star on every row to mark favourites; the favourites go in a section at the top of the list (not while searching). The window size is remembered between sessions and instances. These are kept in a small settings file in the user's application-data folder, apart from the project state.
- **Interface:** the switches of an amp (channel, bright, mode...) with two to four short positions are now a row of buttons with the chosen one lit, and one click picks a position; switches with long position names keep the drop-down.
- **Interface:** an amp can group its knobs under section titles (`"group"` in the `ui` entries of `amp.json`) and mark the knobs a selector makes useless (`"active_when"`: selector name and the positions where the knob works); the editor draws one titled block per group and dims the knobs that are not active. German Trio uses both (CLEAN, CRUNCH / LEAD, OUTPUT; the channel you are not on and the master you are not using are dimmed), with full labels in place of the "CL ..." abbreviations. All the amps now use it: their knobs sit in blocks (PREAMP, TONE, OUTPUT, CHANNELS, and CLEAN / LEAD / EFFECTS and so on where the amp has them), and in the amps with a CHANNEL switch (British Dual 100, German Ultra, German Trio) the knobs of the channel you are not on are dimmed.
- **RESONANCE on the American 5153** (a new knob next to PRESENCE): the 5150 power amp's resonance network (a 500k pot from the 8 ohm tap and 6n8 into the feedback node, through 22u), as an assumption because the 5153's presence-board sheet was not available. It is a low-frequency feedback control (about 1 to 2 dB at 80 Hz in the small-signal response); below about 50k the loop is unstable, so the pot stops there.
- **"German Trio"** (a 100 W three-channel EL34 head with an ECC83 preamp, from the service manual drawing of the preamp and of the power amp, with the DC voltages of the real amp written on it): a shared first stage (V1A) and three channels picked by relays (selector CHANNEL): **Clean** (its own tone stack with GAIN, TREBLE, MIDDLE, BASS and VOLUME, a BRIGHT switch, then V1B), **Crunch** and **Lead** (562k / 2.2n into a 100k or 250k GAIN pot, V2A, V2B, V3A, the V3B cathode follower, and a second tone stack with TREBLE, MIDDLE, BASS and VOLUME; the two channels differ in the gain pot and in two resistors of the stack, so the plug-in has one set of knobs for both). After the channels come V4A (a cathode follower with the effects loop in bypass), V4B, a DEEP network and two master volumes chosen with the selector MASTER (the relays MA1 / MA2), a long-tailed ECC83 phase inverter whose feedback (from the 8 ohm tap) is high-frequency only (2.2n and 27k4) with PRESENCE shunting it, and four EL34 on a 550 V supply with their own 415 V screen supply and a fixed bias for about 27 mA per tube. The DC points of the model match the voltages written on the schematic within a few volts (V1A plate 185 V, V2A 246 V, V2B 347 V, V3B cathode 193 V against 200 V, V4B 265 V). Assumed or not modelled: the effects loop (send and return normalled, the return pot at its middle), the separate PRESENCE pots of the channels (one knob here), the 4 and 16 ohm taps, the bias trimmer and the BY509 grid clamps; the output transformer is 2.4k plate-to-plate, which gives the 160 W the data sheet states as maximum. The preamp is solved in five stages (the guitar and V1A with the input networks, the Clean channel, V2A / V2B, V3 with the tone stack, and the output stages), and the stages of the channels that are not heard are skipped. Levelled at the reference with the Clean preset; not listened to.
- **"German Trio II"** (the second three-channel 100 W EL34 head of the same maker, from the service manual drawing of its preamp and power amp): the same chain as the German Trio (V1A shared, Clean through V1B, Crunch and Lead through V2A / V2B / V3A / V3B, V4A / V4B, DEEP, two masters, the same ECC83 long-tailed phase inverter and four EL34) with its own tone stacks. **Clean** has a different EQ in front of the gain pot (220p and 100k from the contact, HIGH 250k, LOW 250k and MID 25k as variable resistors with 100n and 47n) and the BRIGHT switch; **Crunch** and **Lead** share one set of knobs (the drawing repeats the same stack with the same values) with a 22n / 10k input network, MID 100k, LOW 1M with 1n across each half, HIGH 1M with 2.2n and VOLUME 1MA, and a new selector BASS BOOST for the 4.7n switch of the LOW pot. Not modelled: the effects loop (in bypass), the three presence pots (one knob), the MIDI channel switching and the 4 and 16 ohm taps. Levelled at the reference like the other amps; not listened to.
- **Eight more languages for the interface: Portuguese (Brazil), Italian, Simplified Chinese, French, German, Japanese, Russian and Korean** (Español and English were there). The language menu of the editor lists them, and Auto follows the language of the system (pt, it, zh, fr, de, ja, ru, ko). Parameter names (what a host shows for automation) and the names of the amps and cabinets stay in English. The translations were written without a native speaker: corrections are welcome. The tabs of the editor are as wide as their text needs.
- **The amps with a transistor output stage (the Modern Bass 800 and the American Clean 120) show power transistors where the tubes are** (the panel is "POWER TRANSISTORS"), drawn in VectorCraft (`plugin/tubes/transistor.vectorcraft`); how many are drawn is the amp.json power option `output_transistors`.
- **Multi impulse loader** (cabinet mode MULTI in the first cabinet): up to eight impulse responses mixed with a cursor on a plane. Every impulse is a point on the plane (drag it to move it, double click to put it back); the closer the cursor, the more of that impulse is heard, with the total power kept the same, and SPREAD sets how far each point reaches. Each impulse has its own level, solo, polarity (Auto makes the biggest swing positive, or + / -), delay (0 to 3 ms) and high / low-pass, baked into the impulse when it changes, so they cost nothing while playing; an impulse nobody hears is not convolved at all. "In phase" lines all of them up with the first one (polarity and time offset from their cross-correlation) and shows what it did to each. The impulses and their settings are saved with the project. The cabinet choice gets a new last entry, Multi (the second cabinet plays its filter for it, as it does for NAM).
- **"In phase" for the cabinet's two impulses** (a switch in the A / B blend panel of each cabinet, parameter `ir_align`, off by default so old projects sound the same): two impulses recorded with different microphones arrive at different moments, and one of them may be upside down, so mixed they cancel part of each other. With the switch on, the cross-correlation of their direct sound gives the offset and the polarity that make them add up; the earlier one is delayed, B is flipped when needed, and the panel shows what it did (for example "B flipped, A +0.15 ms").

### Changed
- **The NAM models run about twice as fast on Windows**: the NAM core (Eigen) is compiled with AVX2 and FMA (MSVC `/arch:AVX2`), which every x86 CPU since 2013-2015 has; a standard WaveNet model went from 386 % to 183 % of a core in the benchmark before the optimisation flags of the local build were fixed, and 33 % after.
- **The cabinet's DYNAMICS no longer darkens the sound as it goes up**: the cone break-up cut the peaks of the treble and so its level, on top of the loss of level of the other two effects (at 10 the treble was about 0.9 dB further down than the rest). The level of that band is now given back slowly, so only its shape changes (rounder peaks, more edge) and the balance between bass and treble stays about where it was (0.5 dB at the most, on a pushed JCM800); the overall compression of the control is as before.
- **The cabinet uses far less CPU** (a cabinet with impulses went from about 11 to under 1 point of a core per impulse): the convolution was told it could get blocks of 4096 samples whatever the host sends, so it worked in FFTs about nine times bigger than needed. It is now prepared for the host's own block size (a power of two) and cuts any bigger block into pieces of that size; the impulse length no longer matters much.
- **American 5153 solves only the channel you hear**: the tubes of the other channels (the Red channel's input and V5 / V6 stages when Green or Blue is selected; the Green / Blue stages when Red is) are skipped, a new amp.json option (`jacks` on a preamp stage, `main_jacks` for the first netlist). The sound is the same sample for sample (a test checks it); Green and Blue go from about 42 % to 27 % of a core in Normal and from 30 % to 14 % in Lite.
- **German Ultra, British Super Lead, Modern BE and the SVT also skip the tubes of the channel that is not heard** (the same option, now also by selector position: `when_selector`). A test goes through every jack and selector combination of the five amps and checks the sound is the same as with every stage solved. In the clean channel of the German Ultra the preamp does about 45 % of its work, in the clean channels of the Super Lead and Modern BE about 75 %.
- **British 900 uses about half the CPU**: the clipping network at the second gain stage was five diodes (two paths of three in series sharing one) and is now two devices (each way three identical diodes in series, one diode with three times the thermal voltage: the same current at every voltage). The preamp solves with 11 device ports instead of 14 and 7,700 instead of 17,100 cycles per sample.

## [0.5.0-beta] - 2026-11-13

### Fixed
- **The Gritty Distortion overdrive model runs at four times the host rate** (it was twice): its hard clipping folded harmonics back as an inharmonic fizz about 45 dB under the note (about 43 dB with TONE open); it is now about 59 dB under (54 with TONE open). The plates glow with the plate current and the tube throws a warm halo over the panel; the glow exaggerates a change from what the tube idles at, so when the amp is pushed the tubes flare up (the plates burn white-hot) and settle back as it eases.
- **The amp and the cabinet can be removed** (the button of their unit, or right click on the tile), and put back from the **+** menu (Amp, Cabinet): the rack plays with no amp or no cabinet, a saved project without them loads without them, and in a parallel the first amp and cabinet stay in lane A while the second ones are always in lane B. Only the first noise gate stays. (A parallel needs an amp or a cabinet to build lane A around.)
- **The American Bass 300 has presets for a bass guitar**, instead of the guitar ones: Clean, Warm tube, Motown round, Deep Ultra Lo, Slap scoop, Rock grind, Overdriven and Both channels, measured with a low tone and a hotter pickup, with its Ultra Lo / Ultra Hi switches and mid frequency set for each.
- **Factory presets for every amp** (all but the NAM loader), from a **Presets** button in the amp's unit: **Clean**, **Edge of breakup**, **Crunch** and **Lead**. Each one sets the channel (jack), the knobs of the panel and the switches of the amp (the Boutique's overdrive trim, the Mod BE's SAT...), and they follow the amp that is selected. They are in each `amp.json` ("presets": a name, a jack, "params" on the panel's 0..10 scale and "selectors") and were worked out by measuring how hard the amp is driven (`sas_amp_presets`: the compression of a guitar-level sine against a very quiet one, about 1, 3, 7 and 13 dB), so the gain knob lands where the amp really clips; a preset that an amp cannot reach (a Twin does not get to a 13 dB lead) sits at the nearest end of the knob.
- **Factory presets for every module that had none**, from the **Presets** button of its unit: the noise gate (6), compressor (7), overdrive (8), phaser (8), chorus (8, with the two Juno buttons), vibe (7), tremolo (8), flanger (8), pitch shifter (8: octaves, fifths, half and whole step down...), clean boost (8) and limiter (7). Choosing one sets every knob of the module and switches it on; "Off" only switches it off and keeps the knobs (plugin/ModulePresets.h). The delay, reverb, tape echo, fuzz, wah and EQ keep their own.
- **Right click on a tile of the rack** opens a menu: **Remove** (the ends of a parallel ask about the parallel) and **Replace with**, which puts another module in the same place and lane, at its defaults and switched on. The amps, the cabinets and the first noise gate stay; a type at its limit and the module's own type are greyed out.
- The **modulation effects with a wet/dry blend inside (phaser, chorus, vibe, flanger) have a Mix knob** (`ph_mix`, `ch_mix`, `vb_mix`, `fl_mix`, 0 to 100 %). At 100 % (the default, so old projects sound the same) the effect is as it always was; below that the dry signal comes back. The tremolo has none: its depth already is the mix.
- **Spanish interface**, detected from the language of the system (any Spanish; every other language gets English), and a **Language** choice (Auto, English, Español) in the Presets menu. It is saved in the user's application data folder (`Schematic Amp Sim/language.txt`, shared by the plugin and the standalone) and applies the next time the editor window is opened. Texts come from `plugin/I18nEs.cpp` through `tr("...")`: a text with no translation shows in English. Parameter names (what the host shows for automation) and the names of the amps and cabinets stay as they are.
- A **modular rack**. Everything but the tuner is now a module of an ordered rack: the amp (with its condition and speaker dynamics), the cabinet, noise gate, compressor, overdrive, NAM pedal, phaser, chorus, vibe, pitch, EQ, delay and reverb. The noise gate, the amp and the cabinet are always there (they cannot be removed; the gate and the cabinet can be switched off from their tile, the cabinet giving the amp's raw signal); a **+** button adds the rest (up to twelve besides the amp; several of most kinds, so two overdrives or two reverbs), they are reordered by dragging or with the arrows (a reverb before the amp, a delay in front of the cabinet), and removed with the x. Reordering fades the output for a few milliseconds so nothing clicks, and a module keeps its state (a reverb its tail) when it moves. Each unit shows its CPU use; a mono module after a stereo one says it sums to mono.
- **Tremolo** and **Flanger** (Modulation menu of the rack), up to two of each. The tremolo has sine, triangle and rounded-square waves, rate, depth and a stereo auto-pan; the flanger a swept short delay with rate, depth, manual (centre of the sweep) and feedback from -90 to +90 %, with a quarter-cycle offset between the ears on a stereo pair. Both work on a mono or a stereo signal, wherever the player puts them (host ids `tr_*`, `fl_*`).
- Every modulation module (phaser, chorus, vibe, tremolo, flanger) has a **Channels: Mono / Stereo** choice (`*_stereo`). Mono (the default, so old projects sound the same) gives both channels of a stereo pair the same modulation; Stereo puts the right channel's sweep out of step with the left's (half a cycle in the phaser, vibe and tremolo, which then pans; a quarter in the flanger; the chorus CE-2 sweeps the other way; the Juno chorus is always two voices). It only has something to work on where the signal is stereo, that is after the cabinet.
- The **delay** and the **reverb** have the same Channels choice (`dly_stereo`, `pl_stereo`). Delay: Mono (the default, as it always was) or Stereo, where the left repeats come at three quarters of the time of the right ones, so the two ears hear different echoes (ping-pong is stereo already). Reverb: Stereo (the default, as it always was) or Mono, the same tail on both sides.
- **Wah** (Effects menu), up to two. A resonant band-pass that sweeps between a **Heel** and a **Toe** frequency (both free), with Q, mix, level and a Mono/Stereo choice. What moves it is your choice: **Pedal** (a position you can automate or map to a controller in the host), **Envelope** (an auto-wah that follows how hard you play, with sensitivity, attack, release and Up/Down) or **LFO** (it rocks by itself, with a rate). The panel shows only the controls of the chosen mode. The wah has 10 factory **presets** (Classic pedal, Vox style, Throaty lead, Cocked wah, Subtle, Funk quack, Envelope down, Snappy auto-wah, Slow sweep, Stereo swirl; choosing one switches it on, "Off" switches it off). A new wah goes in front of the compressor and overdrive that sit next to the amp (`wah_*` host ids).
- **Fuzz** (Effects menu), up to two, in DSP at twice the host rate. Three types: **Face** (an asymmetric, furry soft clip; Bias starves it until it gates and sputters), **Muff** (two clipping stages and a tone stack with the mid scoop in the middle of the knob) and **Octave** (a rectified signal that adds an octave above). Fuzz, Tone, Bias, Mix (the dry guitar blended in, kept in phase with the oversampling latency) and Level, and 8 factory presets (`fz_*` host ids). Like the Tube Screamer it is mono, loads the guitar in front of the amp and goes in front of the compressor and overdrive next to the amp.
- **Clean Boost** and **Limiter** (Effects menu), up to two of each, on a mono or stereo signal. The boost is a plain gain of 0 to 30 dB with a tone tilt around 800 Hz (up to 6 dB either way) and an optional soft clip for when it runs out of headroom. The limiter is a brick wall with a 1 ms lookahead (reported to the host as latency): Gain pushes the signal in, Ceiling is the loudest it can ever be, Release how fast the gain comes back; on a stereo pair both channels get the same gain. A new boost goes in front of the amp, a new limiter at the end of the chain (`bst_*`, `lim_*` host ids).
- **Tape Echo** (Space menu), up to two: a loop of tape with three playback heads at 1, 2 and 3 times the Time, and a Heads choice for any of the seven combinations. Intensity is the feedback (past 100 % the repeats swell and self-oscillate, bounded by the tape saturation), Tone is how many highs the loop carries (each repeat is darker and thinner), Saturation drives the record head, Wow and Flutter wobble the tape speed so the repeats bend in pitch, and changing Time glides the pitch like a real tape. Mono or Stereo (the heads spread left, middle, right) and 7 factory presets (`tape_*` host ids). It rings out when switched off.
- The **equaliser has eight bands** instead of five: three more bells (`eq6_*` to `eq8_*`, at 500 Hz, 2 kHz and 10 kHz, each with its own frequency range, gain and Q). The first five are as they always were (band 1 the low shelf, band 5 the high shelf), so old projects and presets sound the same; the new ones start flat, and the factory presets leave them flat.
- The extra instances have their own host parameters (`ts_drive__2`, `pl_decay__2`...); the first instance of each kind keeps the ids it always had, so automation keeps working.

### Changed
- **Choosing another amp puts that amp's own power tubes back**: a tube type picked for one amp (6L6, EL34...) no longer follows the player to the next amp; the new amp starts with its stock tubes. It applies to amp A (the list) and to amp B (its selector).
- The **cabinet keeps a stereo pair**: when a stereo signal reaches it (a stereo effect in front of it), each channel goes through the same filter, NAM model or impulse response and it stays stereo; before, the pair was summed to mono first. With two impulses the blend applies to both channels (the A/B width, which makes stereo out of a mono signal, only works on a mono one). A mono signal behaves as it always did.
- A module that is added or put in the place of another **starts switched on**; before, a new pedal or effect started off.
- **Preamps split into small circuits** for the American Bass 300 (SVT), the American Lead 100 and the British Mod 100: the same circuits, solved in three or four parts instead of one, which takes the CPU of those amps down by a third to a half (for instance the Bass 300 on a hot lead from 48 % to 35 % of a core, the Lead 100 from 65 % to 32 %). The level and tone match the whole circuit within half a dB; the Bass 300's second channel loses about 3 dB at 110 Hz, because the two channels no longer share a supply filter.
- **Parallel paths**: the + menu has **Parallel**, which adds a splitter and a mixer. The signal splits into lane A (the first amp and cabinet) and lane B, which starts empty (a dry path): the player decides what goes in it, from the **Lane B** submenu of the + menu — a second amp with its own cabinet, or just a reverb, a delay, a pedal... Either lane can hold any module, and they are mixed with a level, a pan and a phase switch per lane; the mixer lines the two lanes up in time (their latencies are compared and the earlier one is delayed), and a lane whose amp is still being built plays nothing and comes in smoothly. A module before the splitter or after the mixer is shared; one inside a lane is for that amp only (a compressor and an overdrive right next to an amp still run inside it). The second amp has its own parameters (`volume__2`...), circuits, condition and impulse responses; its cabinet has no NAM mode (it plays the filter) and a NAM Loader amp in lane B plays the model of the first one. The header shows the total CPU of the rack. Projects without a parallel are unchanged.
- A new instance opens in the **Efficient** quality (projects keep the quality they were saved with).
- The standalone window can be maximised, and the editor shows the product icon.
- The editor has two pages, **Rack** and **Tuner**; the Amp, Cabinet, Effects, Pitch, EQ, Mod and Space pages are units of the rack. The **amp condition** (tubes, bias, noise, wear, unit) and the speaker dynamics are part of the amp unit.
- A compressor and an overdrive right in front of the amp (in that order) still run inside the amp's own front end, so the sound of existing projects does not change.
- The Pre / Post switches of the phaser, chorus and vibe are gone: they are positions in the rack (the old values are read once to place the modules of an old project).

### Migrated
- Projects and presets saved before the rack (without a `<RACK>` node) open as they were: the rack is built from the old parameters, with the same order, and renders the same sound (checked against reference renders). It holds the modules that were on or had their knobs touched, as bypassed modules if they were off, plus the gate, the amp and the cabinet; a switch that an old project automated but that was untouched in the saved state has no module to act on until you add one.

### Known limitations (new in this version)
- The new amps were transcribed from the users' drawings and measured, not compared with the real amplifiers; their output levels were set by measurement and the tone is not tuned by ear.
- The American Lead C+ has no reverb, no Simul-Class switch and no mods of the C++ kind; the American Clean 120's power amplifier is one op-amp (not the discrete stage); the American 5153's feedback, presence and resonance board is assumed.
- The Klon-style drive has no charge pump (fixed rails) and an assumed germanium diode model; the spring reverb's tail does not ring out when the module is switched off.
- The automated tests were not run for this version.

It runs at the host rate; a model trained at another rate is flagged as off-pitch. Uses the Neural Amp Modeler Core (MIT), fetched by CMake into `third_party/` and never copied into the repository.

Normal stays as it was. Measured in `docs/PLAN_CPU.md`.

## [0.2.0-beta] - 2026-10-28

### Added
- A harmonic exciter ("maximizer") on the EQ page, after the EQ: a BASS amount adds harmonics of the lowest band (under 140 Hz) and a TREBLE amount adds harmonics of the
  highest (over 3.5 kHz), each with a gentle shelf. The harmonics follow the band's own level, so they are the same percentage at any playing level. Both at 0 is bit-exact bypass.
  It does not do the time alignment of the three bands that a hardware maximizer does.
- A post equaliser on its own EQ page: five bands (low shelf, three bells, high shelf) plus a high-pass and a low-pass, after the cabinet and the double tracking, with a curve to drag

- A pitch shifter on its own Pitch page: -12 to +12 semitones and -50 to +50 cents, in front of the whole chain (the tuner still listens to the raw input). It is the
  granular two-head shifter of the earlier Rust octave plugin, with a 20 ms grain; it adds about 12 ms of delay, reported to the host, only while a shift is set.
- A new amp, British Or, from a 1970s 120 W head schematic (preamp, output amp and power supply drawings): the Orange tone circuit, a six-position FAC
  switch (shown as the input list), four EL34 and 15k feedback with a BOOST control. Which EL34 pair drives which end of the transformer is not drawn;
  it is chosen so the feedback is negative. The jack levels of the FAC positions are only partly matched (the amp is saturated). See `amps/orange_or120/*.net`.
- The amp list is scrollable and has a search box above it.
- A new amp, British Hi-Fi, from a 100 W high-headroom head owner's manual (preamp issue 4, output stage, power supply): brilliant and normal channels, passive
  tone stack with master volume, four EL34 and a silicon bridge supply. The printed -38 V bias idles 53 mA per tube with this tube model, so the source
  is set for -43 V (about 38 mA). The plate voltages printed on the preamp are 40 to 75 V lower than the model's; the 100 pF between the preamp's
  last plate and the presence pot is left out (see the comments in `amps/hiwatt_dr103/*.net`).
- A new amp, American Bass 50, from the 5F6-A tweed bass head schematic (Kuehnel's redrawing): bright and normal channels with #1 and #2 inputs,
  two 6L6GC, tube rectifier and choke. The bias source and the rectifier are tuned, not read, because the drawing prints no idle current.
- A new amp, American Triple Stack, from a three-channel solo head schematic (preliminary drawing): channel 1 Clean / Pushed, channels 2 and 3 with
  Raw / Vintage / Modern voicings, four 6L6GC. The relays, the effects loop and the per-channel presence feedback are not modelled; the voicings
  are interpretations of the drawing (see the comments in `amps/triplestack_3ch/*.net`).
- A macOS build (VST3 and Audio Unit, one universal binary for Apple Silicon and Intel, macOS 11 or later), packaged by `package-macos.sh`.
  Not tried on a real Mac yet and not notarized.
- GitHub Actions: `ci.yml` builds and tests on Windows and macOS on every push; `release.yml` builds both and creates a draft release when a
  `v*` tag is pushed.

### Changed
- The output transformers have winding capacitance (150 pF across the primary) and core loss (about 21 times the plate-to-plate impedance), set per amp in
  `transformer_parasitics`: a roll-off and a resonance in the treble. The core loss also damps the resonance: without it the 120 W high-gain amp rings at rest
  with its feedback loop, and 300 pF rings even with it.
- The power amps drive a reactive speaker load (voice-coil resistance and inductance, cone resonance) instead of a plain resistor: the output now has a bass bump, a mid dip
  and a rising top like a tube amp into a real speaker. The Lite fits and the output levels of every amp were redone. Lite cannot reproduce the bump and the dip
  (worst fit error about 9 dB on the small amps). Each amp can opt out with `speaker_load.enabled = false`.
- The input jack selector has 8 positions (it had 4); saved projects keep their selection. Amps list order is unchanged, the new amp is last.
- Strict floating point (`-ffp-contract=off`) on clang and gcc, as `/fp:precise` on MSVC.

## [0.1.0-beta] - 2026-10-27

The first public release: a Windows VST3 with seven circuit-level amplifiers.

### Amps
- Seven amps, each a netlist transcribed from a schematic and solved as a nonlinear circuit in real time: British 800, British Plexi,
  SLSH 100W, American Twin (optical vibrato and spring reverb), American 5050, British Class A and American Tweed.
- Quality modes Normal (2x), High (4x), Lite and Eco, with level matching across amps, jacks and modes.
- Output transformer core saturation, tube and solid-state rectifier supplies with sag, a choke on the Class A amp.
- Data-driven: a new amp is a folder with `amp.json` and its netlists; nothing is recompiled except to embed them.

### Realism
- Output tube choice (EL34, 6L6GC, 6550 where the amp is fixed-bias) re-biased to the same share of the tube's rating, a bias control with
  the plate current read from the circuit, noise and hum that depend on the pickup, wear, and a "unit" number that draws component tolerances.
- Guitar input: single coil, humbucker or P-90 and cable capacitance in front of every amp.
- Glowing power tubes in the editor that follow the plate current.

### Cabinet and stereo
- Cabinet off, a simple filter, or two impulse responses (A and B, each with its response curve) with an equal-power blend; stored in the project.
- Speaker dynamics between the amp and the cabinet: cone excursion, voice-coil heating and break-up, with an Intense mode.
- Fake stereo: impulse A on the left and B on the right (Width), automatic double tracking (Double and Time). A mono track can feed the
  stereo output.

### Effects and tools
- Compressor pedal solved as a circuit from the Keeley Compressor Plus schematic (an OTA gain cell, a transistor envelope detector and the
  feedback that closes the loop), a Tube Screamer style overdrive, a noise gate.
- Chromatic tuner with a needle meter, a reference pitch of 430 to 450 Hz and an option to mute the output.

### Editor
- A list of amps on the left, four pages (Amp, Cabinet, Effects, Tuner), colours and knobs that change with each amp, level meters.

### Known limitations
- Windows (VST3) only. AU and AAX are planned.
- The tube, transformer, rectifier, pickup and noise values are typical figures, not measurements of a particular amp; the schematics
  print none. They are documented next to each netlist.
- The Lite and Eco modes use the stock amp: the output tube choice, wear and unit act in Normal and High only.
- The tuner detects from 60 Hz to about 1.4 kHz.
- While the compressor or the overdrive is on, the pickup and cable of the Guitar Input panel have no effect.
- The saved-project format may still change between 0.x versions.
